Aboveground fuel tanks

An aboveground storage tank (AST) is any fuel tank that sits on or above the ground instead of buried: a farm tank on a stand, a skid tank at a job site, a generator's base tank, or a fleet's fueling tank. Here's how they're built, the rules that apply, and the equipment that goes on them.

Types of aboveground tanks

Most steel aboveground fuel tanks are built to UL 142. Protected tanks are built to UL 2085, which adds insulation between the walls for fire protection (Western Global).

TypeWhat it isTypical use
Single-wall (UL 142)One steel wallNeeds a separate dike or containment area to meet spill rules (40 CFR 112.8(c)(2))
Double-wall (UL 142)A tank inside a tank; the outer wall catches a leak from the inner oneCan help meet the containment requirement, but still counts toward your SPCC capacity (see the SPCC guide)
Protected (UL 2085)Double-wall with insulation between the walls, built to limit heat to the inner tank during a 2-hour fire; can also be tested for ballistics and vehicle impact (Western Global)Permanent installations with higher fire risk, such as gasoline dispensing; some states require it for aboveground gasoline dispensing tanks (NC State Fire Marshal)
Transfer and auxiliary tanksSmall tanks in a pickup bedSee diesel transfer tanks

Field note: On the farm and fleet sites I service, most tanks are UL 142, and double-wall tanks are more common than single-wall tanks in a dike. Protected tanks are heavier and harder to move than standard tanks (Western Global).

The rules at a glance

Three sets of rules can apply to an aboveground tank: the federal SPCC rule, your fire code, and your state's tank rules.

RuleWho it coversGuide
SPCC (40 CFR 112)Sites with more than 1,320 gallons of aboveground oil storage that could spill to water; farms have higher thresholdsDo I need an SPCC plan?
Fire code (IFC or NFPA 30/30A, as adopted locally)Tank construction, spacing, and equipment such as anti-siphon valvesAnti-siphon valves
State tank rulesVaries by state; some register or inspect aboveground tanksCheck with your state fire marshal or environmental agency
