Fuel quality

Aviation fuel quality control: sumping, filters, and DEF

By Austin, fuel-system technician/8 years in the trade/Updated November 2, 2026.

Aviation fuel quality control comes down to keeping water, dirt, and the wrong fluid out of the aircraft: sump and check samples every day, read and record filter differential pressure under flow, change filter elements at their pressure or age limit, and keep diesel exhaust fluid (DEF) far away from jet fuel additives. The detailed rules are industry standards such as ATA 103 and NFPA 407; this guide follows the U.S. Department of the Interior's 2024 fuel handbook, which applies them (DOI Aviation Fuel Management Handbook).

"Clear and bright" means no visible solids, cloud, haze, emulsion, or free water, with the sparkle of clean, dry fuel in transmitted light (DOI handbook). It's the standard every sample is held to.

Daily checks

  • Sump samples: take samples from tanks, filter sumps, and nozzles and check them for clear and bright. If you can't get a clean, dry sample after three samples, take the unit out of service (DOI handbook).
  • Filter differential pressure: check and record it under normal flow. A sudden rise or drop means a problem: stop fueling and take the unit out of service to investigate (DOI handbook).
  • Nozzle pressure can't exceed 50 psig at constant flow (DOI handbook).
  • Deadman controls and safety interlocks get a function check; a refueler shouldn't be able to move with a nozzle out of its stowed position (DOI handbook).
  • Hoses, nozzles, and swivels: check for leaks, cracks, soft spots, blisters, and exposed reinforcement, and make sure dust caps are on (DOI handbook).

Field note: As long as people sump daily, I don't find much on inspections. Daily sumping is what keeps these systems clean.

Filter differential pressure limits

Differential pressure across the filter tells you how loaded the elements are. Take the unit out of service if it's over these limits (DOI handbook):

Filter typeMaximum differential pressure
Filter/separator15 psi
Filter monitor (water-absorbent)15 psi
Dirt defense filter15 psi
Water barrier filter22 psi
  • The limit scales with flow. The 15 psi limit is at rated flow; at 50% of rated flow, the limit is 7.5 psi (DOI handbook). Read it at the same flow each day, such as in recirculation, so readings compare.
  • Watch the trend. A steady rise means elements are filling up. A sudden drop to zero means an element ruptured: shut the system down (DOI handbook).
  • Use qualified filters. Aircraft refueling equipment needs filtration qualified to EI 1581 (filter/separators) or EI 1583 (filter monitors). EI-qualified spin-on filters are no longer made for aviation use (DOI handbook).

Field note: On the systems I service, we change filters annually.

Annually, the handbook also calls for opening filter vessels to check that the interior is clean and free of water, sediment, and microbial growth; checking pressure gauge accuracy to within ±2% of full scale; calibrating meters; and testing the water defense system (DOI handbook).

Keep DEF out of jet fuel

This is a real, recent hazard. Between November 2017 and May 2023, there were multiple events where fueling staff put DEF into a refueling truck's fuel system icing inhibitor (FSII) reservoir by mistake, and it was injected into jet fuel. Mixed with jet fuel, DEF forms crystals that clog fuel filters and nozzles; several aircraft had to make emergency landings (FAA CertAlert 23-04).

Both fluids are clear and come in similar white plastic containers. The FAA recommends (FAA CertAlert 23-04):

  • Don't store DEF and FSII near each other
  • Mark bulk containers with 4-inch or larger stenciled letters visible from all sides: "DIESEL DEF" and "JET FUEL SYSTEM ICING INHIBITOR"
  • Lock DEF in a separate shed or cabinet, with the key only for ground vehicle maintenance staff
  • Don't refill FSII containers on fueling vehicles; use replacement containers when possible
  • Remove and discard jet fuel suspected of DEF contamination; don't repurpose it
  • Only trained people add fluids to aviation equipment and aircraft

The FAA's airport fueling advisory circular builds DEF contamination prevention into required fuel safety training topics (FAA AC 150/5230-4C). For storing DEF itself, see how to store DEF.

For pilots

Before flight, pilots sump the aircraft per the manufacturer's instructions and FAA guidance, draining at least 10 ounces from each sump until it's clear and bright, and confirm the fuel source is marked with the right grade (DOI handbook).

Records

Log differential pressure and flow rate every day. Reviewing past entries is how you spot a filter trending toward its limit (DOI handbook). Keep sump, filter change, and inspection records with the equipment.

FAQ

What color should aviation fuel be?

100LL avgas is blue, grade 100 avgas is green, and Jet A is clear to straw (DOI handbook). Any of them should be clear and bright, with no haze, particles, or water.

When do filter elements have to be changed?

When differential pressure hits the limit, when the element reaches its service life, or both. The DOI handbook caps filter monitor and water barrier elements at 12 months and coalescer elements at 36 months (DOI handbook). Follow the element maker too.

Can DEF-contaminated jet fuel be used in something else?

No. The FAA says to remove it, discard it according to the regulations, and not repurpose it for other aircraft or vehicles (FAA CertAlert 23-04).
