How long does diesel last in storage?

By a fuel-system technician/8 years in the trade/Updated October 29, 2026.

Diesel kept clean, cool, and dry can generally be expected to last 6 to 12 months (Alberta Agriculture). Chevron's guidance for emergency fuel is to use it within a year and replace it with fresh fuel, unless special precautions are taken (Chevron, via Truck News).

That's a rule of thumb, not an expiration date. Well-kept fuel with a stabilizer and regular testing can go much longer, and fuel sitting on a water bottom in a half-empty tank can go bad faster. The diesel spec itself, ASTM D975, has no storage-stability test for #1 or #2 diesel (NREL), so testing is how you know for sure.

What happens to diesel as it sits

Stored diesel breaks down in four ways, and most of them start with water or air.

ProblemWhat it doesWhat speeds it up
OxidationFuel reacts with air and forms gums, sludge, and acids that plug filtersHeat, air in a half-empty tank, and metals like copper, brass, and zinc (NREL)
WaterCollects on the tank bottom from condensation and leaksA low fuel level and caps or vents that don't seal (CRC Report 672)
MicrobesBacteria and fungi grow in the water layer, make slime, and plug filters (Gilbert)Water on the bottom and warm weather
Wax settlingIn cold weather, wax can settle into a layer on the bottomFuel stored below its cloud point (CRC Report 671)

Biodiesel blends need more attention. Most diesel sold today can contain up to 5% biodiesel. A B20 blend meeting the minimum spec is expected to keep its quality for up to a year in clean, dry storage, and fuels with better stability or added antioxidants lasted much longer in testing (NREL). Antioxidants only slow aging; they can't reverse it, so they work best added when the fuel is fresh (NREL).

How to tell if stored diesel is still good

Pull a sample from the bottom of the tank into a clear jar and look at it, then check the bottom for water. Good diesel is "clear and bright" with no haze, particles, or water layer (CRC Report 672). Sample jars: clear fuel sample bottles on Amazon.

What you seeWhat it likely means
Clear and bright, no waterFuel is fine to use
Hazy or cloudy at room temperatureWater suspended in the fuel
Water layer at the bottom of the jarFree water in the tank; drain it (see water in your diesel tank)
Dark color, slime, or stringy materialMicrobial growth (see diesel bugs and biocides)
Sediment or dark particlesSludge from aging fuel or rust; expect filter plugging

Sample from near the bottom, not the top. Water, microbes, and sediment all collect at the bottom, so a top sample can look fine while the bottom isn't (Bell Performance). Check for a water layer in the tank itself with water-finding paste on a gauge stick: water-finding paste on Amazon.

For fuel that has to work, test it. Standby generators are the clearest case: NFPA 110 requires a fuel quality test at least once a year using ASTM methods, and says tanks should be sized so fuel is used within its storage life (Bell Performance on NFPA 110). A fuel lab can test water and sediment, stability, and microbes. If fuel fails, the usual fix is to recondition or replace it, clean the tank, and change filters (Massachusetts fire code excerpt).

How to make stored diesel last

Keep it full, cool, dry, and treated, and use the oldest fuel first.

  1. Keep the tank nearly full. Less air space means less condensation and less oxidation (Alberta Agriculture).
  2. Keep it cool and out of the sun where you can. Heat speeds up aging (Alberta Agriculture).
  3. Drain water off the bottom on a schedule, at least monthly and after deliveries (see water in your diesel tank).
  4. Add a stabilizer when the fuel is fresh, and a biocide if microbes have been a problem. Filtering the fuel periodically and adding stabilizers and biocides help store diesel longer (Alberta Agriculture). Diesel stabilizers on Amazon.
  5. Avoid copper, brass, and zinc in the fuel system. These metals speed up fuel breakdown, especially with biodiesel blends; carbon steel, aluminum, and stainless are better (NREL).
  6. Size the tank to what you use. A tank you empty a few times a year stays fresher than one that holds a two-year supply. For standby generators, that's an NFPA 110 requirement, not just good advice (Bell Performance on NFPA 110).
  7. Rotate it. Burn the old fuel before adding a big load of new fuel to it, so old fuel doesn't keep getting mixed into fresh.

What about gasoline?

Gasoline goes bad much faster. Alberta Agriculture recommends using gasoline within about a month of purchase, and expects it to stay good for at least 6 months under good storage conditions (Alberta Agriculture). A gasoline stabilizer helps.

FAQ

Is 2-year-old diesel still usable?

It might be, if it was stored well. Test it first: check a bottom sample for water, haze, and growth, and have a lab test it if it matters. Don't run it in critical equipment untested.

Does off-road (red) diesel last longer than on-road diesel?

The dye doesn't change how it ages. Storage conditions, water, and biodiesel content matter much more.

Can I mix old diesel with new?

Small amounts of good old fuel can be blended into fresh fuel and burned. Fuel with water, slime, or sludge should be cleaned up or removed first, or it will contaminate the fresh fuel.

Do fuel stabilizers really work?

Antioxidant stabilizers slow aging but can't reverse it, so add them while the fuel is fresh (NREL).
