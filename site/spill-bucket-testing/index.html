Spill bucket testing: what the UST rule requires and how the test works

Written by a fuel-system technician. Updated October 28, 2026.

A spill bucket is the container around an underground tank's fill pipe that catches fuel that drips when the delivery hose comes off. Under the federal UST rule, you check it every 30 days and test it for tightness at least every 3 years, unless it's double-walled and you check between the walls instead (EPA; 40 CFR 280.35).

Spill buckets are meant to hold small spills for a short time, not to store fuel. EPA says to empty them right away and dispose of what's inside properly (EPA).

What the federal rule requires

Any UST filled with more than 25 gallons at a time needs spill and overfill prevention (EPA). For spill buckets, that means three ongoing jobs:

RequirementHow oftenDetails
Walkthrough inspectionEvery 30 days, or before each delivery if deliveries are less frequentCheck for damage, remove liquid or debris, clear any obstruction in the fill pipe, make sure the fill cap is secure, and on double-walled buckets check between the walls for a leak (EPA)
Tightness test (single-wall)At least every 3 yearsVacuum, pressure, or liquid test to show it's liquid tight (40 CFR 280.35)
Interstitial monitoring (double-wall)At least as often as walkthroughsReplaces the 3-year test. Stop monitoring and you have to test within 30 days (40 CFR 280.35)

Keep the records. Test records are kept for 3 years. If you skip testing because the bucket is double-walled, keep proof that it's double-walled for as long as you monitor it (40 CFR 280.35). Keep the most recent year of walkthrough records (EPA).

Deadlines. Tanks in use on or before October 13, 2015 needed their first test by October 13, 2018. Newer tanks have to comply from installation (40 CFR 280.35).

EPA also recommends checking the bucket before and right after each delivery, and after rain or snowmelt (EPA).

How a spill bucket test works

The test has to follow the manufacturer's procedure, a recognized code of practice such as PEI RP1200, or your state's method (40 CFR 280.35; Georgia EPD). Either way, a bucket with a visible crack or hole fails without testing (Georgia EPD form).

MethodHow it's donePasses if
Hydrostatic (water)Fill the bucket with water to within 1.5 inches of the top and hold it at least an hourThe level drops less than 1/8 inch (Kubinsky, National Tanks Conference 2022; Georgia EPD)
VacuumSeal a test lid on top and pull 30 inches of water column vacuumVacuum holds at 26 inches or more (Kubinsky)
Vacuum on the interstitial space (double-wall)Pull 15 inches of water column between the wallsHolds at 12 inches or more (Kubinsky)

Field note: I test with water. While the bucket is open, I always check the drop tube gasket and the fill gasket, and replace them if they're bad or stretched.

The water from a hydrostatic test has been in contact with fuel residue, so handle and dispose of it properly.

What fails, and what to do about it

Cracks, bad seals, leaking drain valves, and broken lids are the usual problems. EPA says to repair or replace any of them right away (EPA).

ProblemWhy it matters
Cracked, damaged, or corroded bucketNot liquid tight; it won't hold a spill (EPA)
Worn seals or gaskets, including around the fill riserThe bucket leaks even if the body is fine (EPA)
Damaged drain valveLets fuel or water into the tank, or out of the bucket (EPA)
Damaged, missing, or loose lidLets water and debris collect in the bucket and get into the tank (EPA)

Keep it clean and dry. Water left sitting in the bucket can get dumped into the tank through the drain valve. That's one of the ways water gets into stored fuel (see water in your diesel tank).

State rules

States run most UST programs, so your state's rules are the ones you're inspected on. Michigan, for example, adopted the same 3-year test for spill prevention equipment, with the same double-walled option (Mich. Admin. Code R 29.2120). Some states publish their own test forms (Georgia EPD).

FAQ

Can I just drain the fuel in my spill bucket back into the tank?

Many buckets have a drain valve for that, but water and debris go in with it. EPA's advice is to empty the bucket right away and dispose of the contents properly (EPA).

Does a double-walled spill bucket ever need testing?

Not while you check between the walls at least as often as your walkthroughs. If you stop monitoring, you have 30 days to test it (40 CFR 280.35).

Who can test a spill bucket?

The federal rule sets the method, not who does it. Some states require a certified tester and others don't, so check with your state UST program.

Do aboveground tanks need spill buckets?

The federal UST rule covers underground tanks. For aboveground tanks, see the aboveground fuel tanks guide and your fire code.
