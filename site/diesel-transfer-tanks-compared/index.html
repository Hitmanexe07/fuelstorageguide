Diesel transfer tanks compared: sizes, weights, and specs

By Austin, fuel-system technician/8 years in the trade/Updated November 2, 2026.

Pick a transfer tank by three things: what your truck can carry full, whether it needs to be approved for gasoline, and the shape that fits your bed. Diesel weighs about 6.9 pounds a gallon (EPRI), so a full 100-gallon steel tank weighs over 800 pounds. Stay at 119 gallons or less for diesel to avoid bulk hazmat rules (J.J. Keller).

Payload: the number that decides your size

Find the payload on your door-jamb sticker, then subtract everything else you carry. From the table above:

  • A 50-gallon aluminum tank is about 400 pounds full, which most half-ton pickups can carry with room to spare.
  • An 80-gallon tank runs about 628 pounds full in aluminum and 698 in steel, a 70-pound difference.
  • A 100-gallon steel tank is about 832 pounds full, before the pump, hose, and tools.

Add the pump, hose reel, toolbox contents, and passengers. If the total is close to your payload, go smaller or go aluminum.

Diesel-only vs. gasoline-approved

This is the spec that matters most if you'll ever haul gasoline.

  • RDS lists its aluminum tanks as "DOT approved" for diesel, gasoline, or aviation fuel (RDS 80-gallon listing).
  • Better Built says its tanks are for combustible fuels such as diesel, are "SOLELY FOR PRIVATE USE and not for interstate commerce or commercial applications," and that their design does not meet Title 49, the federal transportation rules (Better Built catalog).
  • Transfer Flow's 80-gallon tank above is listed for diesel (Transfer Flow listing). Transfer Flow makes separate gasoline tanks under a DOT special permit (see the rules guide).

If you use the tank for work, check that its approvals fit commercial use, not just private use.

Features to check

  • Baffles to cut sloshing; all the tanks above list them
  • A rollover vent. RDS lists a rollover safety valve. Better Built says its tanks must be vented at all times (Better Built catalog)
  • Fittings: 2-inch fill and pump openings are standard on these tanks; a bottom drain makes water removal easy
  • A level gauge, standard on some RDS models
  • Mounting hardware included; never strap a tank in loose

FAQ

What's the best size transfer tank?

The biggest one your truck's payload can carry full, up to 119 gallons for diesel. Over 119 gallons is bulk packaging and needs placards (J.J. Keller). See the full-weight table above.

Can I put gasoline in any transfer tank?

No. Only in a tank approved for it. RDS lists its aluminum tanks as DOT-approved for diesel, gasoline, or aviation fuel (RDS 80-gallon listing). Better Built says its tanks are for combustible fuels like diesel and don't meet DOT regulations for transportation (Better Built catalog).

Steel or aluminum?

Aluminum is lighter and doesn't rust; steel usually costs less. On a half-ton pickup, the weight difference can decide what size you can carry.
