UST walkthrough inspection: the 30-day and annual checklist

By Austin, fuel-system technician/8 years in the trade/Updated October 31, 2026.

Under the federal UST rule, you walk your system every 30 days to check spill buckets and release detection, and once a year to check containment sumps and hand-held release detection equipment. Keep a record of each walkthrough for one year. The requirement has applied since October 13, 2018 (40 CFR 280.36).

The walkthrough is the routine check that catches problems between the formal tests: a cracked spill bucket, a loose fill cap, an alarm nobody noticed. It doesn't replace the 3-year tests or the yearly line leak detector test; it runs alongside them.

Who has to do walkthroughs

Owners and operators of regulated underground storage tanks. Some tanks aren't USTs under the federal rule, including farm or residential tanks of 1,100 gallons or less holding motor fuel for noncommercial use, and tanks holding heating oil used on the premises (40 CFR 280.12).

States run most UST programs, so your state's version of the rule is the one you're inspected on. You can meet the walkthrough requirement any of three ways (40 CFR 280.36(a)):

  • Check the equipment the federal rule lists (the checklist below)
  • Follow a recognized code of practice; the rule names PEI RP 900, the Petroleum Equipment Institute's recommended practice for UST inspection and maintenance
  • Use a walkthrough developed by your state program

Every 30 days

EquipmentWhat to check
Spill prevention equipment (spill buckets)Visually check for damage; remove liquid or debris; check for and remove obstructions in the fill pipe; make sure the fill cap is securely on the fill pipe; on double-walled equipment with interstitial monitoring, check for a leak between the walls
Release detection equipmentMake sure it's operating with no alarms or other unusual operating conditions; make sure release detection test records are reviewed and current

Source: 40 CFR 280.36(a)(1)(i).

Field note: On walkthroughs I find all of it: water or fuel sitting in spill buckets and sumps, fill caps that aren't tight, and alarms nobody acted on. Another gap I see a lot is owners not printing the sensor status reports from the tank monitor, so there's no record of what the sensors showed.

Infrequent deliveries. If your tank gets fuel less often than every 30 days, you can check the spill prevention equipment before each delivery instead. You then keep the delivery records with your walkthrough records (40 CFR 280.36). Release detection still gets checked every 30 days.

For what a spill bucket should look like and why water in it matters, see spill bucket testing. For release detection on pressurized piping, see line leak detectors.

Once a year

EquipmentWhat to check
Containment sumps (under dispensers and at the tank top)Visually check for damage, leaks into the sump, or releases to the environment; remove liquid (in contained sumps) or debris; on double-walled sumps with interstitial monitoring, check for a leak between the walls
Hand-held release detection equipmentCheck devices such as tank gauge sticks or groundwater bailers for operability and serviceability

Source: 40 CFR 280.36(a)(1)(ii).

Records you have to keep

Keep walkthrough records for one year. Each record has to include (40 CFR 280.36(b)):

  • A list of each area checked
  • Whether each area was acceptable or needed action
  • A description of what was done to fix any problem
  • Delivery records, if you check spill equipment before deliveries instead of every 30 days

Field note: The slip-up I see most is owners who do the walkthrough but never write it down. Without the record, there's nothing to show an inspector.

Keep them at the site, immediately available to an inspector, or at another location and provide them when asked (40 CFR 280.34(c)). Records of the 3-year spill bucket, sump, and overfill tests are kept longer: three years (40 CFR 280.35(c)).

FAQ

Who is allowed to do the walkthrough?

The federal rule doesn't say who has to do it, only what gets checked and what gets written down. Your state may set its own requirements, so check with your state UST program.

Does my farm tank need walkthroughs?

Not if it's 1,100 gallons or less and holds motor fuel for noncommercial use. Those tanks aren't USTs under the federal rule, and neither are heating oil tanks used on the premises (40 CFR 280.12). Your state may still regulate them.

Do I send walkthrough records to the state?

No. Keep them at the site where an inspector can see them right away, or at another location and hand them over when asked (40 CFR 280.34(c)).

My tank only gets fuel every couple of months. Do I still check spill buckets every 30 days?

No. If deliveries come less often than every 30 days, you can check spill prevention equipment before each delivery instead. Keep the delivery records to show why (40 CFR 280.36).
