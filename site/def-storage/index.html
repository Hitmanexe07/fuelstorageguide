How to store DEF: shelf life, freezing, and bulk tanks

By Austin, fuel-system technician/8 years in the trade/Updated November 2, 2026.

Store diesel exhaust fluid (DEF) sealed, out of the sun, and between about 12°F and 86°F. Kept cool, it lasts a year or more; at 86°F and up, figure on about six months. It freezes at 12°F and expands about 7%, so don't let a full tank freeze, and keep it in a dedicated tank made of stainless steel or plastic (Cummins; API).

What DEF is

DEF is 32.5% urea and 67.5% deionized water, used by engines with selective catalytic reduction (SCR) to cut NOx emissions (Cummins). It has to meet the ISO 22241 quality standard (Navistar). It's nonflammable and nontoxic, weighs about 9.1 to 9.2 pounds per gallon, and has a light ammonia smell (Navistar; John Deere).

Look for the API certification mark. For bulk deliveries, API suggests asking your supplier for a certificate of analysis with each shipment (API). Never put DEF in the diesel tank (John Deere).

Shelf life by temperature

Heat is what shortens DEF's life. Navistar's service guidance gives the clearest numbers (Navistar):

Storage temperatureExpected shelf life
23–77°F (−5 to 25°C), sealed, out of the sunAt least 18 months
86°F (30°C)About 12 months
95°F (35°C)About 6 months
Above 95°FSignificant loss; check quality before every use

Other makers say much the same. Cummins recommends storing DEF between 12°F and 86°F, and says shelf life extends to 2 years below 65°F (Cummins). API is more conservative at the hot end: about six months at 86°F or higher, and 12 months or longer stored properly (API). Sunlight shortens it too (API).

Plan for the hot end if your storage gets hot. A tote in a sunny metal shed in July isn't stored at 65°F.

Freezing

  • DEF freezes at 12°F (−11°C). At its 32.5% concentration, the urea and water freeze and thaw together, so it comes back at the right mix (Cummins).
  • It expands about 7% when frozen and can damage a tank that's full or nearly full when it freezes. Keep storage tanks less than full in winter (API).
  • Don't add anything to keep it from freezing. No anti-gel or freeze-point additive should ever go in DEF (Cummins). Equipment DEF tanks have heaters that thaw it after startup (API).

Bulk tanks and totes

  • Dedicate the tank to DEF. If you switch a tank to DEF, rinse it thoroughly with distilled or deionized water first (API).
  • Use the right materials. Most plastics, highly alloyed steels, stainless steel, and titanium are fine. Mild iron and non-ferrous metals such as zinc, aluminum, magnesium, and nickel aren't (Navistar). Hoses, fittings, and seals need to be DEF-approved, such as stainless, certain plastics, or EPDM rubber (Cummins). Don't reuse a fuel pump or hose.
  • Keep it sealed and out of the sun (Navistar; John Deere).
  • Use dedicated dispensing equipment. No funnels, pitchers, or hoses that have touched other fluids. Clean with distilled or deionized water, then a DEF rinse; never tap water (API).
  • Rotate stock and use the oldest DEF first (API).

Field note: The problem I see most on DEF setups is crystallization at the dispensing nozzle, when the nozzle isn't kept in water between uses. If you keep the tip in water, use distilled or deionized water, since tap water contaminates DEF (Navistar).

Size your storage to your use

DEF use averages about 3 to 5% of diesel use (Cummins). That's the key to sizing:

Diesel used per yearDEF used per year (3–5%)A 330-gallon tote lasts about
2,000 gallons60–100 gallons3+ years: longer than the shelf life
5,000 gallons150–250 gallons1–2 years
10,000 gallons300–500 gallons8 months to a year

Field note: Most of the farms I service store DEF in totes.

A small farm can easily buy more DEF than it will use before it ages out. Deere's advice is simply to buy amounts you can use in a reasonable time (John Deere). Smaller containers or more frequent deliveries can beat a big tote.

Spills

DEF isn't flammable, but a spill left to dry leaves a white urea residue. Clean it up properly; left on equipment, it can be mistaken for a leak from the dosing system (Navistar).

FAQ

Does DEF go bad?

Yes, slowly, and faster when it's hot. API says properly stored DEF can last 12 months or longer, and about six months if stored at 86°F or higher (API). Check the date on containers and use the oldest first.

Can I add anything to keep DEF from freezing?

No. Don't add anti-gel or anything else; it will keep DEF from working and can damage the system (Cummins). DEF thaws at the right concentration, and equipment with SCR has heaters for the DEF tank (API).

How do I know if DEF is bad?

Good DEF is crystal clear with a light ammonia smell. Don't use it if it's cloudy, tinted, or smells strongly of ammonia (John Deere). A DEF refractometer checks the urea concentration.

Can I rinse a DEF tank or funnel with the hose?

No. Tap water contaminates DEF. Rinse with distilled or deionized water, or rinse with tap water and then with DEF (Navistar).
