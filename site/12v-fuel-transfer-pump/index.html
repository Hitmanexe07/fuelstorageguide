How to choose a 12V fuel transfer pump

Written by a fuel-system technician. Updated October 28, 2026.

For a pickup transfer tank or a small farm tank, a 15 GPM pump is the usual fit. Step up to 20 GPM for bigger tanks and frequent fueling, and drop to about 8 GPM for drums and occasional use. Then check three things before you buy: the duty cycle, the fuels it's rated for, and whether your truck or battery can feed its amp draw.

Most 12-volt pumps can't run all day. The common Fill-Rite and GPI models are rated for 30 minutes on, then 30 minutes off (Fill-Rite FR4210H; GPI M-150S). That's plenty for fueling equipment, but it rules them out for long, continuous transfers.

What the specs mean

Five numbers on the box decide whether a pump fits your job.

SpecWhat it tells youWhat to look for
Flow rate (GPM)Maximum gallons per minute with nothing slowing it downReal flow is lower once you add a filter, meter, long hose, or auto nozzle
Duty cycleHow long it can run before it needs to cool"30 on / 30 off" is typical; some small pumps are 15 on / 30 off (GPI EZ8). If it overheats, a thermal switch shuts it off (Fill-Rite manual)
Amp draw and fuseHow much current it pulls from the battery15 to 20 GPM pumps draw about 19 to 20 amps (Fill-Rite FR1210HN; FR4210H). Bigger pumps need heavier wiring
Approved fuelsWhat it's built to pumpMost pumps in this class are rated for diesel up to B20, gasoline up to E15, and kerosene. Check before pumping anything else
Motor listingWhether the motor is certified for use around fuel vaporLook for a UL or cULus motor listing for hazardous locations (GPI M-150S; Fill-Rite sales sheet)

About thermal shutoff: On Fill-Rite's 1200 and 4200 series, once the motor overheats and shuts off, you have to let it cool and then turn the switch off to reset it. Some other series reset on their own (Fill-Rite manual). If your pump quits mid-fill and won't restart, that's often why.

All six are listed for diesel up to B20, gasoline up to E15, and kerosene. The fill times are the best case. Real-world times run longer once you add a filter, meter, and long hose.

  • Fill-Rite's FR4200 series moves a third more fuel than the FR1200 at about the same amp draw (Fill-Rite sales sheet), so it's often worth the step up for a farm tank.
  • 25 GPM pumps pull about two and a half times the current of a 15 to 20 GPM pump (Fill-Rite sales sheet). Plan for heavy wiring and a strong charging system.
  • Fill-Rite's longer warranty is a real difference from GPI's 2 years on these models.

Which pump for which job

JobSize to start withWhy
Drums and occasional small fillsAbout 8 GPMCheaper and lighter, and a 15-minute run is enough for a drum or two
Pickup transfer tank (roughly 50 to 119 gallons)15 GPMFills equipment fast enough, and draws about 20 amps the truck can handle
Farm skid tank or yard tank fueling big equipment20 GPMShorter fill times for large tractor and truck tanks, at about the same amp draw as 15 GPM in Fill-Rite's lineup
High-volume fueling or long transfers25 GPM, or an AC pumpA 12V pump's 30-minute duty cycle becomes the limit; a 120V pump may fit better if power is available

Gasoline needs extra care. The pumps above are rated for gasoline up to E15, but pumping gasoline means more vapor and more fire risk. Use a pump with a hazardous-location motor listing, an unleaded nozzle, a grounded hose, and bond the pump to the tank or frame (Fill-Rite manual; GPI M-150S).

Installing and wiring it

Wire it straight to the battery with a fuse near the battery, use at least 12-gauge wire, and bond the pump to the tank. These come from Fill-Rite's manual for its 1200, 2400, 4200, and 4400 series (Fill-Rite manual); check your own pump's manual for its numbers.

  1. Power from the battery. Red to positive, black to negative. Put the fuse holder as close to the battery as you can. Fill-Rite calls for a 30-amp fuse on 12V installs, 20 amps on 24V.
  2. Wire size. Nothing smaller than 12 gauge. For runs longer than 20 feet, size the wire to your electrical code. Longer runs need heavier wire to avoid voltage drop.
  3. Bond the pump. Use the green bonding screw and wire to connect the pump to the tank or vehicle frame. Never connect power wires to the green screw.
  4. Keep the suction tube off the bottom. Fill-Rite says at least 3 inches up from the tank bottom. That keeps the pump from pulling water and sludge that settle there. See water in your diesel tank.
  5. Put a filter on the outlet. It keeps dirt and water out of the equipment you're fueling (Fill-Rite manual). Fill-Rite's standard transfer filters are 10 micron, with water-detecting versions available (Fill-Rite filters).
  6. Skip extra check or foot valves unless they have a built-in pressure relief. They can cut flow (Fill-Rite manual).

Common problems and fixes

From Fill-Rite's troubleshooting guide (Fill-Rite manual):

ProblemCheck, in this order
Slow flowDirty inlet screen; leaks or kinks in the suction line; a sticking bypass valve; a plugged filter, hose, or nozzle; worn rotor and vanes; low fuel in the tank
Won't primeLeaks or blockage in the suction line; bypass valve stuck open; nicked or worn vanes. For vapor lock, shorten the distance from pump to fuel and remove the automatic nozzle to test
Motor won't runPower at the pump; a failed switch; loose or wrong wiring. If the motor or thermal protector has failed, return it; don't open the motor
Quit mid-fillLikely the thermal shutoff after running past the duty cycle. Let it cool; on Fill-Rite 1200 and 4200 series, turn the switch off to reset

A plugged filter is one of the most common causes of slow flow at farm tanks, and it often means water or microbes in the tank.

FAQ

What size wire does a 12V transfer pump need?

At least 12 gauge, with a fuse near the battery. Fill-Rite specifies a 30-amp fuse for its 12V pumps, and heavier wire for runs over 20 feet (Fill-Rite manual).

Can I run a 12V pump continuously?

Most can't. The common 15 and 20 GPM pumps are rated 30 minutes on, 30 minutes off (Fill-Rite FR4210H; GPI M-150S).

Can I use the same pump for gasoline and diesel?

The models here are rated for both, but don't switch back and forth on the same tank. Mixing gasoline into diesel lowers the flash point and creates a fire hazard (EPRI).

Does a bigger pump mean faster fueling?

Up to a point. Your hose, filter, meter, and nozzle all limit flow, and a bigger pump draws more current from your battery.
